# Type 2 Diabetes Treatment Classes for Evidence Briefings

This note is for research analysts synthesizing cardiometabolic evidence. It is not clinical advice.

First-line foundation remains lifestyle change plus metformin in many guidelines, with earlier use of organ-protective agents when atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease (ASCVD), heart failure (HF), or chronic kidney disease (CKD) is present. Two drug classes dominate contemporary outcomes evidence:

- SGLT2 inhibitors (empagliflozin, dapagliflozin, canagliflozin, ertugliflozin): oral agents that increase urinary glucose excretion. Consistent benefits on heart-failure hospitalization and kidney disease progression; cardiovascular death benefit is class-heterogeneous and clearest in some dedicated CVOTs.
- GLP-1 receptor agonists (liraglutide, semaglutide, dulaglutide, and related peptides): injectables or oral semaglutide that enhance incretin signaling. Typical effects include substantial HbA1c lowering, weight loss, and reduction in major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE) in selected CVOTs. Dual GIP/GLP-1 agonists (tirzepatide) add further glycemic and weight effects; dedicated cardiovascular outcome data were still maturing relative to the older CVOTs.

Insulin and sulfonylureas remain relevant for glycemic control but are not the main organ-protection story in modern outcomes trials. DPP-4 inhibitors are generally glycemic agents with largely neutral CVOTs.

When an analyst is asked which add-on is “best,” the question must be decomposed: glycemic potency, weight, HF, CKD, ASCVD, hypoglycemia, gastrointestinal tolerability, cost, and administration route. No class wins every axis.

Regulatory labels and professional society consensus (for example ADA/EASD) should be treated as living documents. A briefing should cite the specific trial population rather than extrapolating from HbA1c studies to event reduction.
